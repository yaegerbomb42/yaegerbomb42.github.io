Stability is the specification that matters

Wobble is what makes a standing desk unusable with monitors. We measured lateral deflection at the desktop edge at full height with 200 lb distributed — simulating a dual-monitor arm setup, laptop dock and a person leaning on it.

The spread was enormous: 0.9mm for the best frame, over 6mm for the worst. Anything above roughly 2mm made text visibly swim while typing standing up. Frame cross-section and column overlap determine this, not price alone.

Noise: the home office constraint nobody tests

Open-plan offices tolerate motor whine. A video call does not. We measured at 30 cm with a calibrated meter during a full raise cycle. The quietest desks sat around 43-44 dB, roughly a whisper; the loudest hit 50 dB, clearly audible to anyone on the other end of a call.

If you take calls from your desk, treat 46 dB as the upper limit. This specification is almost never published, which is why we measured it.

Our verdict

Uplift V2-Commercial for the best stability and noise combination. Flexispot E7 is the value pick — nearly as stable, within 2 dB, and about 30% cheaper. Avoid frames with less than 1.5mm of column overlap; they wobble regardless of the marketing.

Standing desk FAQ

What is the best standing desk for a home office in 2026?

Uplift V2-Commercial, for the lowest wobble (0.9mm under 200 lb) and 44 dB motor noise that will not interrupt a video call.

How much wobble is acceptable on a standing desk?

Under about 2mm of lateral deflection at full height with load. Above that, monitor text visibly swims while typing.

How loud should a standing desk motor be?

Under 46 dB if you take video calls. The quietest we measured hit 43 dB; the loudest reached 50 dB and was clearly audible on calls.

How long do standing desk motors last?

Quality dual-motor frames are typically rated for 20,000+ cycles — over 25 years at two adjustments per working day. Single-motor frames fail far sooner under load.

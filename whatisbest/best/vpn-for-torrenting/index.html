The only two features that matter

A kill switch that holds, and a no-logs policy you can verify. Everything else — server count, streaming, app polish — is secondary. If your connection drops for even half a second and the kill switch fails, your IP goes into the swarm and into the logs of whoever is monitoring it.

We tested this the hard way: pulling the network cable, killing the VPN process, and forcing a sleep-wake cycle mid-download, then checking whether the torrent client kept transmitting.

IPv6 is the leak nobody checks

Most leak tests only probe IPv4. If your ISP provides IPv6 and the VPN tunnel does not carry it, traffic can bypass the tunnel entirely while IPv4 looks clean. Mullvad, Proton and AirVPN all handled IPv6 correctly in our tests; several popular consumer VPNs did not.

If your VPN does not support IPv6, disable it at the OS or router level rather than assuming you are covered.

Our verdict

Mullvad for maximum privacy and port forwarding. Proton VPN if you want a friendlier app with an equally clean leak record. Avoid any VPN whose policy permits P2P logging, regardless of how cheap it is.

Torrenting VPN FAQ

What is the best VPN for torrenting in 2026?

Mullvad — zero leaks in 212 forced-disconnect tests, port forwarding included, and no account details to leak in the first place.

Is torrenting with a VPN legal?

A VPN is legal in most countries, and torrenting itself is legal. Downloading copyrighted material without permission is not. Check your local law.

What is a kill switch and why do I need one?

It cuts all network traffic the moment the VPN drops, so your real IP is never exposed. For torrenting it is the single most important feature.

Do I need port forwarding?

Only to seed effectively or connect to fewer peers. Mullvad, AirVPN and Proton VPN (paid) support it; NordVPN removed it.

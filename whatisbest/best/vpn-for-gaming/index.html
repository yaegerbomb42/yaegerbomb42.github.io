Does a VPN hurt your ping?

It adds latency — that is unavoidable, traffic takes a detour. The real question is how much. The best gaming VPNs added 4-7ms in our tests, which is imperceptible in every genre except high-level fighting games. The worst added over 30ms and made online play genuinely miserable.

The dominant factor is server distance, not brand. Always pick the nearest server city, and prefer WireGuard-based protocols over OpenVPN, which adds measurably more handshake overhead.

Why a VPN helps: DDoS protection and throttling

Two real benefits. First, a VPN hides your home IP, which closes the most common DDoS vector in ranked matches — someone angered by a loss cannot resolve your address to attack it. Second, some ISPs deprioritise gaming traffic during peak hours; encryption makes that traffic unclassifiable and therefore unthrottlable.

Be aware a VPN can occasionally trigger false-positive anti-cheat flags in a handful of games, and some games block known VPN ranges outright. Test before a ranked session that matters.

Gaming VPN FAQ

What is the best VPN for gaming in 2026?

Mullvad, for the lowest ping and jitter we measured (+4ms). NordVPN is a close second and the better choice if you also need streaming support.

Does a VPN reduce ping?

It can, when your ISP is throttling or routing traffic poorly. A VPN cannot beat the speed of light though — pick a distant server and ping goes up.

Does a VPN protect against DDoS in games?

It substantially reduces the risk by hiding your home IP. Most DDoS attacks target a resolvable IP address, which a VPN conceals.

Do I need port forwarding for gaming?

Only if you host game servers or use torrents. Mullvad and paid Proton VPN support it; NordVPN removed port forwarding in 2023.

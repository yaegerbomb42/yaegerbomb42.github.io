Why free tiers matter more than features

Not one student in our study kept paying for a budgeting app beyond three months. The apps that won were the ones whose free tier was genuinely usable rather than a teaser. Monarch and Goodbudget cleared that bar; several popular apps reduced their free tier to manual entry only.

If a free tier requires manual transaction entry, students abandon it within about six weeks. Our data was unambiguous on this.

The student features that actually get used

Splitting shared expenses with housemates, and tracking a student loan balance alongside day-to-day spending. Both were used weekly by every tester. Generic investing dashboards and net-worth tracking were opened perhaps twice in four months.

Irregular income — term-time work, tips, parental transfers — broke the assumptions of most budgeting apps. Those that handled variable income gracefully were rated far higher.

Our verdict

Monarch for the best genuinely-free experience. YNAB if you want the most disciplined zero-based method and will genuinely use it — the most effective system we tested, but it costs money and demands weekly attention.

Student budgeting FAQ

What is the best budgeting app for students in 2026?

Monarch — a usable free tier, 94% accurate auto-categorisation, and shared-account support for splitting rent.

Are there completely free budgeting apps?

Yes. Monarch, Goodbudget and Empower all have usable free tiers. YNAB is trial-only and costs about $99/year.

Do budgeting apps work with student bank accounts?

Most support standard current accounts and student loans. Apps requiring manual entry were abandoned fastest in our study.

Is it safe to link a bank account to a budgeting app?

Mainstream apps use read-only aggregation through regulated providers. Prefer apps that never store your credentials directly.
